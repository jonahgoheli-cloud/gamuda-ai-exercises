1. The word "Catholic" comes from the Greek "katholikos," meaning "universal."

2. The Catholic Church is the world's oldest continuously operating international institution.

3. It traces its lineage back to Jesus Christ and the Twelve Apostles in 1st-century Judea.

4. Saint Peter is considered by Catholics to be the first Pope, given authority in Matthew 16:18.

5. Saint Ignatius of Antioch wrote the earliest recorded use of "Catholic Church" around 107 AD.

6. Catholicism was legalized in the Roman Empire via the Edict of Milan in 313 AD.

7. Emperor Theodosius I made Catholic Christianity the official state religion of the Roman Empire in 380 AD.

8. The Church assembled the canon of the Bible at the Council of Rome (382 AD) and the Council of Hippo (393 AD).

9. Saint Jerome translated the Bible into Latin (the Vulgate) in the late 4th century.

10. Monks in European monasteries preserved classical Western literacy, philosophy, and science through the Dark Ages.

11. The Great Schism of 1054 split the Church into the Western Roman Catholic Church and the Eastern Orthodox Church.

12. The Western Schism (1378–1417) saw up to three rival claimants to the Papacy at the same time.

13. The Council of Trent (1545–1563) sparked the Counter-Reformation in response to Protestantism.

14. The First Vatican Council (1869–1870) formally defined the dogma of Papal Infallibility.

15. The Second Vatican Council (1962–1965) modernized liturgy, replacing standard Latin Mass with local vernacular languages.

16. There are over 1.37 billion baptized Catholics worldwide, making up roughly 17% of the global population.

17. Latin America holds the world's largest Catholic population (~40% of all global Catholics).

18. Brazil is the country with the single largest number of Catholics (~120+ million).

19. The Catholic Church consists of 24 distinct churches: 1 Latin (Western) Church and 23 Eastern Catholic Churches.

20. Eastern Catholic Churches (e.g., Byzantine, Maronite, Chaldean) are in full communion with Rome but maintain distinct liturgical rites.

21. Vatican City is the smallest sovereign country in the world at 0.49 square kilometers (121 acres).

22. Vatican City prints its own stamps, coins (Euros), issues passports, and has its own media network.

23. The Swiss Guard has protected the Pope since 1506; members must be Catholic, single male Swiss citizens with military training.

24. The hierarchy of clergy runs: Deacon -> Priest -> Bishop -> Archbishop -> Cardinal -> Pope.

25. Cardinals under 80 years of age elect a new Pope during a secret meeting called a Conclave inside the Sistine Chapel.

26. Black smoke from the Sistine Chapel chimney means no consensus; white smoke means a new Pope has been elected.

27. The Pope acts in three distinct roles: Bishop of Rome, leader of the global Catholic Church, and Sovereign of Vatican City State.

28. Papal infallibility applies ONLY when the Pope speaks "ex cathedra" ("from the chair") on explicit matters of faith or morals.

29. Papal infallibility has been used explicitly only twice: defining the Immaculate Conception (1854) and the Assumption of Mary (1950).

30. The Holy See holds permanent observer status at the United Nations.

31. Catholics believe in the Holy Trinity: One God existing eternally as Father, Son, and Holy Spirit.

32. The Creed (Nicene or Apostles') summarizes the core dogma of Catholic faith.

33. Catholicism recognizes seven Sacraments: Baptism, Confirmation, Eucharist, Penance (Confession), Anointing of the Sick, Holy Orders, and Matrimony.

34. The Eucharist is considered the "source and summit" of Christian life.

35. Transubstantiation holds that the bread and wine literally become the real Body, Blood, Soul, and Divinity of Jesus Christ during Mass.

36. Catholic Bibles contain 73 books—7 more in the Old Testament (Deuterocanonical books) than standard Protestant Bibles (66 books).

37. Purgatory is understood as a temporary state of purification for souls dying in God's grace before entering Heaven.

38. Mortal sin destroys divine grace within the soul and requires sacramental Confession for forgiveness.

39. Venial sin weakens spiritual grace but does not completely sever a soul's connection with God.

40. Catholic doctrine strictly opposes abortion, euthanasia, assisted suicide, and artificial contraception.

41. Grace is viewed as an unmerited gift from God that requires human cooperation through faith and good works.

42. Original Sin is believed to be inherited from Adam and Eve and is cleansed through Baptism.

43. Catholic teaching asserts that non-Catholics can be saved through God's mercy if they seek truth and follow their conscience.

44. The Church teaches that science and reason are fully compatible with divine faith.

45. Natural Law theology holds that human reason can discern universal moral truths built into the fabric of creation.

46. Mary is venerated as the Theotokos (Greek for "God-bearer" or Mother of God).

47. Catholics DO NOT worship Mary or Saints; they venerate (honor) them and ask for their intercession (hyperdulia for Mary, dulia for Saints, latria for God alone).

48. The Immaculate Conception asserts that Mary was conceived free from Original Sin (often confused with the Virgin Birth of Jesus).

49. The Assumption holds that Mary was taken body and soul directly into Heavenly glory at the end of her earthly life.

50. Canonization is the formal process by which the Church officially recognizes an individual as a Saint in Heaven.

51. The canonization process requires a rigorous investigation of the person's life and usually two verified miracles attributed to their intercession.

52. Saint Joseph, foster father of Jesus, is designated as the Universal Patron of the Catholic Church.

53. Every day of the calendar year is associated with at least one patron saint (the Liturgical Calendar).

54. Saint Jude is known as the patron saint of lost causes.

55. Saint Anthony of Padua is invoked for finding lost items.

56. The Rosary is a scriptural prayer meditative system focused on twenty events ("Mysteries") in the lives of Jesus and Mary.

57. Major Marian apparition sites (e.g., Lourdes, Fatima, Guadalupe) draw millions of pilgrims annually after formal Vatican approval.

58. The image of Our Lady of Guadalupe in Mexico City is the most visited Catholic shrine in the world.

59. Saint Michael the Archangel is considered the chief protector of the Church against spiritual evil.

60. Sacred relics are categorized into 1st Class (body parts of a saint), 2nd Class (belongings), and 3rd Class (objects touched to 1st/2nd class relics).

61. The Catholic Church created the modern university system in medieval Europe (e.g., Bologna, Oxford, Paris, Salamanca).

62. Gregor Mendel, an Augustinian friar, discovered the fundamental laws of inheritance and is the "father of modern genetics."

63. Georges Lemaître, a Belgian Catholic priest, proposed the Big Bang Theory in 1927.

64. The Gregorian Calendar—used worldwide today—was introduced by Pope Gregory XIII in 1582 to correct leap-year errors in the Julian Calendar.

65. Nicholas Copernicus, who posited that the Earth orbits the Sun, dedicated his work "De revolutionibus" to Pope Paul III.

66. The Catholic Church is the largest non-governmental provider of healthcare in the world, managing thousands of hospitals and clinics.

67. The Church operates the largest non-governmental school system globally, with over 100,000 primary and secondary schools.

68. Catholic religious orders founded the modern concepts of organized hospice care and orphanages.

69. Modern international law was heavily shaped by Catholic scholastic thinkers like Francisco de Vitoria and the School of Salamanca.

70. Catholic monasteries pioneered advanced brewing techniques and produced iconic European beers (e.g., Trappist ales) and wines.

71. Champagne was refined by Dom Pérignon, a Benedictine monk in France.

72. The Vatican Observatory (Specola Vaticana) is one of the oldest astronomical research institutions in the world.

73. Major classical composers—including Mozart, Haydn, Beethoven, Vivaldi, and Verdi—wrote sacred music commissioned by the Church.

74. The Renaissance was largely funded by Papal and Catholic patronage of artists like Michelangelo, Leonardo da Vinci, and Raphael.

75. The Sistine Chapel ceiling painting took Michelangelo over four years to complete (1508–1512).

76. Religious priests and monastics take vows of poverty, chastity, and obedience (the evangelical counsels).

77. Diocesan (parish) priests take vows of chastity and obedience to their bishop, but do not take a vow of solemn poverty.

78. The Benedictine Rule (6th century) set the standard motto for Western monasticism: Ora et Labora ("Pray and Work").

79. The Franciscan Order was founded by St. Francis of Assisi in 1209, stressing radical poverty and care for creation.

80. The Dominican Order (Order of Preachers) was founded in 1216, emphasizing theological study and combatting heresy.

81. The Jesuits (Society of Jesus) were founded by St. Ignatius of Loyola in 1540 and are famous for global missions and education.

82. The Missionaries of Charity were founded by Saint Teresa of Calcutta (Mother Teresa) in 1950 to care for the "poorest of the poor."

83. Lent is a 40-day season of penance, fasting, and almsgiving preceding Easter.

84. Catholics abstain from eating meat on Ash Wednesday and Fridays during Lent (and traditionally every Friday of the year).

85. Holy Week includes Maundy Thursday (Last Supper), Good Friday (Crucifixion), Holy Saturday, and Easter Sunday (Resurrection).

86. Advent is the 4-week preparation period preceding Christmas.

87. A scapular is a devotional garment worn around the neck under clothing as a sign of dedication to Mary or a religious order.

88. The Sign of the Cross is made by touching the forehead, chest, left shoulder, then right shoulder.

89. Holy Water is water blessed by a priest used for spiritual cleansing, blessings, and recalling baptismal promises.

90. Incense burnt during Mass symbolizes the prayers of the faithful rising upward to Heaven (Psalm 141:2).

91. St. Peter's Basilica in Rome is built over the historical burial site of Saint Peter the Apostle.

92. The official cathedral of the Pope as Bishop of Rome is SAINT JOHN LATERAN, not St. Peter's Basilica.

93. Gothic architecture (pointed arches, flying buttresses, stained glass) was developed by Catholic monastic builders in 12th-century France.

94. Chartres Cathedral and Notre-Dame de Paris are premier historical examples of high French Gothic Catholic architecture.

95. The Catholic Church is expanding fastest in Sub-Saharan Africa and Asia.

96. The Philippines and East Timor are the only two predominantly Catholic nations in Asia.

97. North America's oldest Catholic city and cathedral are in St. Augustine, Florida (founded 1565).

98. The Sanctuary of Our Lady of Fátima in Portugal hosts millions of pilgrims marking the 1917 Marian apparitions.

99. The Camino de Santiago (Way of St. James) is a historic pilgrimage route leading to the shrine of St. James in Galicia, Spain.

100. St. Peter's Square in Vatican City features an ancient Egyptian obelisk originally brought to Rome by Emperor Caligula in 37 AD.
